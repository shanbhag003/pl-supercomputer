WHAT CHANGED SINCE LAST GAMEWEEK
----------------------------------------------------------
  The model was upgraded this week in two ways: chances a
  team creates while protecting or chasing a lead now
  count at what they would be worth at level, and players
  are now valued partly from their own numbers, which
  sharpens how squad changes count. That moves every club
  a little, so part of each change below is the upgrade,
  not the results.

  Brentford: 4.1 expected points up, title chance +1.4 points, relegation risk -3.4 points
      beat Chelsea 3-0 at home, and rode their luck
  Liverpool: 3.3 expected points up, title chance +1.8 points, relegation risk -0.7 points
      beat Bournemouth 1-0 away
  Ipswich: 3.3 expected points down, relegation risk +14.8 points
      lost 0-1 away at Everton
  Coventry: 3.2 expected points up, relegation risk -10.8 points
      beat Nottingham Forest 1-0 away, and rode their luck
  Nottingham Forest: 2.9 expected points down, title chance -0.4 points, relegation risk +3.0 points
      lost 0-1 at home to Coventry, but played far better
      than the score

  Numbers move for three reasons: results, how well teams
  actually played underneath those results, and any change
  in squad or manager.

HOW IT WORKS
----------------------------------------------------------
  1. Every club gets an attack and a defence score, based on twelve
     seasons of results and the quality of chances created.
  2. Those scores are adjusted for who has arrived, who has left,
     who is injured or suspended, and who is now in charge.
  3. Every remaining match is then played out 20,000 times, and we
     count how often each club finished where.

Deliberately left out: fixture congestion (tested across twelve
seasons - the effect was not consistent enough to trust), and manager
changes where the incoming manager has no Premier League record, since
there is nothing to measure them against.
